About FACETTES

Identity isn’t a single portrait; it’s an ever-shifting collage of how we see ourselves and how others see us. FACETTES turns that collage into a living set of masks.

How it works

Someone—-you, a friend, a total stranger—chooses ten adjectives: curious, intense, gentle, loud.

Each word locks onto a fixed spot inside a 5 × 5 tile grid.

The tiles flip, mirror, and take on one high-contrast color, and in a heartbeat they fuse into a new face: one facette—a tiny polished plane of a much bigger gem.

Why it matters

Susan Sontag wrote that queer people often live with “double awareness”: we act and, at the same time, watch ourselves acting. I feel that constant self-monitoring. At Beit Hayedidut, a hostel for autistic adults, I met people who simply are, unfiltered by outside judgment. Their freedom made me ask: Which parts of me come from inside, and which are stitched together by other people’s words?

FACETTES stages that question. Each mask shows how language from the outside can redraw a face in an instant. Scroll the gallery and you’ll find dozens of facettes—playful, dissonant, surprising. None of them is the whole truth, yet together they form a bright, shifting mosaic of identity.
